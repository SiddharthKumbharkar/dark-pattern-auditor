STRICT INSTRUCTIONS — follow all of these:

1. Use only the evidence and screenshots provided. Do not invent facts,
   prices, claims, or UI elements that are not present in
   evidence_summary.json or the attached screenshots.

2. Use exactly one of these four verdicts — never a bare true/false claim:
   - "fires": the evidence unambiguously supports this attribute being
     present.
   - "unsure": evidence is suggestive but not conclusive either way.
   - "no": evidence does not support this attribute being present.
   - "not_assessable": there is not enough evidence of the right KIND to
     judge this attribute at all. This is different from "no" — "no"
     means you checked and it is clean; "not_assessable" means you
     could not check.

3. A verdict of "fires" or "unsure" MUST be accompanied by at least one
   entry in evidence_pointers naming the specific step_id/selector/signal
   it rests on. Never return "fires" or "unsure" with an empty
   evidence_pointers list — if you cannot point at anything specific, the
   correct verdict is "no" or "not_assessable" instead.

4. Every evidence_pointers entry needs a ui_region, in addition to
   step_id/selector/signal. evidence_summary.json's tagged_elements list
   is the COMPLETE, real (step_id, selector) -> region vocabulary for
   this payload — the ONLY source of truth for what region a specific
   element belongs to. Before writing a named (non-"unclassified")
   region for a pointer, find that exact (step_id, selector) pair in
   tagged_elements and copy its region verbatim. If the exact pair you
   want to cite is NOT listed in tagged_elements — including anything you
   only noticed by looking at a screenshot — you MUST write
   "unclassified". Never invent or guess a region for an element just
   because it visually resembles one used elsewhere: a named region that
   contradicts what an element was actually tagged (or wasn't tagged at
   all) gets your entire response rejected — "unclassified" is always
   the safe, correct choice when you are not reading a region straight
   out of tagged_elements.

5. severity is an integer 1-5, required when verdict is "fires", and MUST
   be null for every other verdict. Scale it by how clear-cut and how
   consequential the instance is, not by how many pieces of evidence you
   cite.

6. confidence is a number from 0.0 to 1.0 reflecting how sure you are of
   the VERDICT itself (not the severity) — calibrate it honestly; do not
   default to a fixed number regardless of how strong the evidence is.

7. reasoning must explain WHY you reached this verdict in terms of the
   specific evidence/screenshots you were given — not a restatement of
   the definition above. IMPORTANT — visual claims: if reasoning
   describes what a screenshot visually shows (e.g. "clearly legible",
   "visually more prominent", any color/contrast/layout description),
   you MUST do at least one of the following, or your response will be
   rejected at ingest:

        "screenshot_visual_inspection" (e.g.

        step_id/selector the visual claim is actually about, or

   If no screenshots were attached to this payload at all (see the
   Screenshots section below), do not make any visual claim in reasoning
   — describe only what evidence_summary.json's measured fields show.

8. Do not let this attribute's definition bleed into a different one.
